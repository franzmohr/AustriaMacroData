\section*{Overview}

This document describes the variables contained in \texttt{FRED-Data.csv}, a
quarterly panel of U.S. macroeconomic series following the \emph{FRED-QD}
format designed by McCracken and Ng (2020), ``FRED-QD: A Quarterly Database
for Macroeconomic Research,'' Federal Reserve Bank of St.\ Louis \emph{Review}.
FRED-QD is the quarterly companion to FRED-MD and is designed to emulate the
dataset used in Stock and Watson (2012), ``Disentangling the Channels of the
2007--2009 Recession,'' NBER Working Paper No.\ 18094.

For each series the file's first three rows encode metadata read directly by
this dataset's construction scripts:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Row 1} -- the FRED mnemonic (column header).
  \item \textbf{Row 2 (``factors'')} -- a 0/1 flag indicating whether Stock
    and Watson (2012) used the series when estimating their factor model
    (1 = yes, 0 = no).
  \item \textbf{Row 3 (``transform'')} -- the recommended transformation
    code for the series $x_t$:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item 1: no transformation
      \item 2: $\Delta x_t$ (first difference)
      \item 3: $\Delta^2 x_t$ (second difference)
      \item 4: $\log(x_t)$
      \item 5: $\Delta \log(x_t)$ (log first difference, i.e.\ approximate growth rate)
      \item 6: $\Delta^2 \log(x_t)$ (log second difference)
      \item 7: $\Delta(x_t/x_{t-1} - 1.0)$
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

If the FRED mnemonic does not end in ``x'' the series comes directly from the
FRED database (e.g.\ \texttt{PCECC96}, real PCE). Otherwise the series is a
modified variant of a FRED series -- most commonly a nominal series manually
deflated by the PCE or core-PCE price index (e.g.\ \texttt{PCDGx}, nominal PCE
durables deflated by the PCE price index). The exception is the S\&P 500
data, which is taken from public sources rather than FRED. Monthly-frequency
source series are aggregated to quarterly frequency by averaging over the
three months of the quarter.

The series below are grouped into fourteen economic categories, following
the original FRED-QD appendix. Within each group, the table reports the
FRED mnemonic, a brief description, the transformation code used in this
dataset (\texttt{FRED-Data.csv}, row 3), and whether the series was among
those used by Stock and Watson (2012) for factor estimation
(\checkmark = yes). These tables, and the source-mapping table that follows
them, are set in landscape orientation for readability.

\section*{Mapping to Austrian Data Sources}

    appendix (McCracken and Ng, 2020), cross-checked against the mnemonics
    actually present in \texttt{FRED-Data.csv}.
  \item A small number of series in this vintage of the dataset use FRED
    mnemonics that superseded the ones documented in the original appendix
    (the underlying concept is unchanged): \texttt{BOGMBASEREALx} succeeded
    \texttt{AMBSLREALx} (St.\ Louis Adjusted Monetary Base), \texttt{M1REAL}
    and \texttt{M2REAL} succeeded \texttt{M1REALx}/\texttt{M2REALx}, and
    \texttt{TWEXAFEGSMTHx} (Trade Weighted U.S. Dollar Index: Advanced
    Foreign Economies) succeeded \texttt{TWEXMMTH} following the Federal
    Reserve Board's 2020 revision of its dollar index series.
  \item Transformation codes (Tcode) and factor-model flags (SW) shown here
    are read directly from rows 2--3 of \texttt{FRED-Data.csv} and may differ
    slightly from the original 2018 appendix vintage as FRED-QD's own
    recommended transformations are occasionally revised.
\end{itemize}
